01 Method

Three steps, and one minute of setup that happens once.

  1. One

    Sign in with Google

    A single consent screen asking for a single permission: the files this app creates. Your existing Drive stays invisible to it.

  2. Two

    Give it your text

    Paste it, or drop a .txt, .md, .docx or .pdf. Set the speed, the typo rate, and how often it stops to think.

  3. Three

    Watch it write

    A new document appears in your Drive and fills in line by line. Pause it, stop it, or come back later and pick up from the character it left on.

02 Detail

Built for how writing actually looks, not how fast it can be pasted.

  • Cadence

    Characters arrive in bursts sized from your target speed, jittered every time, so nothing lands on a metronome.

  • Typos

    Adjacent-key slips, swapped letters, doubled and dropped characters — typed, noticed a beat later, backspaced, retyped.

  • Pauses

    At the end of a sentence, between paragraphs, and at intervals mid-flow. Speed drifts a little from one paragraph to the next.

  • Files

    .docx is unzipped in your browser and never leaves the machine. Text formats are read directly; PDFs go through Drive's own converter.

  • Control

    Pause, stop, resume. If the network drops, one press continues in the same document from the exact character it stopped on.

  • Filing

    Name the document and give it a Drive folder. The folder is created if it does not exist yet.

03 Access

There is no backend. That is the whole design.

The page you load is static files. Your text goes from your browser straight to Google's own API, using a token you grant that expires within the hour.

It is never sent anywhere else, because there is nowhere else — no database, no analytics, no account system, nothing to breach. The app asks for one permission, drive.file, which Google classifies as non-sensitive: it can see and manage only the files it creates.

Sign-in also reads your name and email, to show which account is connected.

04 Questions

What do I need before I can use it?

A Google account. Press sign in, approve one permission, and you are in — the same flow as any other site with a Google button. Sign in once and you stay signed in on that browser.

And if I want to run my own copy?

Then there is a one-time step for you, not for your visitors: create a free Google Cloud OAuth client ID and drop it into src/config.js. It identifies the app to Google so the traffic runs through your project rather than someone else's.

How long does a document take?

Roughly as long as writing it would. Five thousand characters at 55 words per minute is about twenty minutes, and the app estimates it before you start. Keep the tab in front, since browsers slow timers in background tabs.

Does the version history show the typing?

Yes. Each burst is a separate edit, so Google's own revision history shows the document accumulating over time rather than appearing at once.

Can I close the tab while it runs?

No — the typing happens in the page, so it stops when the tab does. Reopening the app and pressing start continues in the same document from where it stopped.

Is this for cheating on homework?

It types text you already have; it does not write anything for you. But it is worth being straight about it: pacing an edit history so work looks drafted over time is something many schools treat as academic misconduct, whoever wrote the words. That call is yours, and it is worth knowing the rules you are under before you make it.
